## 1. Purpose

Do not review in a vacuum if the user has an internal contract playbook, negotiation posture, or standard paper.

This reference absorbs the useful idea behind enterprise contract-review skills: legal review should reflect both enforceability and the user's business position.

## 2. Priority Order

Use this order:

1. Taiwan law and enforceability constraints
2. User-supplied business objectives and red lines
3. User's internal clause playbook or fallback positions
4. General Taiwan commercial drafting norms

If an internal preference conflicts with Taiwan law or likely enforceability, say so explicitly.

If no playbook exists, do not invent one. Use role-based commercial reasonableness instead.

## 3. Role-Based Review

Always identify which side the user is protecting.

### If the user is the discloser / buyer / client / commissioning party

Bias review toward:

- broader confidentiality protection
- clearer audit / supervision rights where personal data processing is involved
- stronger IP ownership language
- tighter subcontracting controls
- prompt incident notification
- workable but credible penalty and damages preservation

### If the user is the recipient / vendor / consultant / service provider

Bias review toward:

- narrower definitions
- reasonable carve-outs and use rights
- proportionate security obligations
- limited liability and narrower indemnity
- feasible response times
- protection against accidental overreach in IP assignment or non-compete style restrictions

### If the document is intended as a neutral form

Aim for:

- bilateral confidentiality
- balanced termination rights
- symmetric notice mechanics
- clear and specific deliverable / ownership language

## 4. Fallback Positions

When the user asks for negotiation notes or revision strategy, separate findings into:

- `Must fix`
- `Preferred fallback`
- `Acceptable if deal pressure is high`

Examples of fallback reasoning:

- `Must fix`: clause likely invalid, contradicts Taiwan law, or creates major ownership / compliance exposure
- `Preferred fallback`: wording is market-heavy but can be tolerated if pricing or leverage favors acceptance
- `Acceptable if deal pressure is high`: non-ideal but bounded operational friction

Do not overuse "must fix." Reserve it for true legal or material risk.

## 5. Negotiation Posture

When proposing revisions:

- explain why the current term is risky
- propose the narrowest revision that solves the risk
- distinguish legal necessity from negotiation preference

Good phrasing:

- `依台灣法下的可執行性風險，建議改為...`
- `若乙方議價能力較弱，可退而接受...`
- `此處不是明文禁止，但目前寫法對甲方過度不利，建議至少加入...`

Avoid:

- pretending every preferred term is mandatory
- giving aggressive fallback language without explaining tradeoffs

## 6. Reporting Guidance

For `Full Review`, combine statute + posture:

- `法律依據`: statute / principle / or `實務偏好`
- `風險說明`: legal exposure + business downside
- `建議修正`: wording or structural change
- `待確認事項`: leverage, deal value, role, internal standard, or missing attachments

If the user explicitly asks for redline-ready negotiation points, add a short section:

```md
### 談判優先序
- 必改:
- 優先爭取:
- 可視交易條件退讓:
```
